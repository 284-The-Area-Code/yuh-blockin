# YuhBlockin Visual Language

## Style

**Revised 2026-09-30** (was premium stylized 3D — see
`docs/marketing/YUHBLOCKIN_BRAND_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md` §12 for why): premium
2D flat/vector animation with subtle parallax/depth layering.

The target is a contemporary 2026 animated commercial with Caribbean
identity — friendly and approachable in the register of Slack's or
Spotify's explainer-video work, not a kids' cartoon and not full 3D.

Use:

- simplified, restrained vehicle/environment shapes (flat/vector, not
  polished 3D forms)
- bold-but-muted color palette (not pastel, not saturated)
- subtle parallax/depth layering between flat planes (not full 3D depth,
  not photographic depth of field)
- smooth, rigged-feeling movement
- sophisticated composition

Avoid:

- childish proportions
- oversized cartoon eyes
- slapstick
- rubbery/bouncy physics
- random visual effects
- generic AI aesthetics
- fully flat single-plane, static-feeling layouts (some depth/parallax
  should be present)
- drifting back toward photoreal/3D rendering

## Caribbean Identity

Use authentic environmental cues:

- island roads
- tropical vegetation
- Caribbean architecture
- coastal areas
- marinas
- commercial areas
- residential roads
- believable BVI terrain

Do not make every scene look like a tourism advertisement.

## Camera (revised 2026-09-30 for 2D)

In flat 2D, "camera movement" means movement within/across a flat
composition, not true 3D perspective or depth-of-field — e.g. an "overhead
shot" is a top-down flat illustration, and "settling into a different
angle" means cutting to a new flat illustration of the same scene, not a
literal camera repositioning in 3D space.

Prefer:

- scale/zoom moves within a flat composition (2D equivalent of a push-in)
- lateral pans across a flat scene
- parallax between background/midground/foreground layers moving at
  different speeds
- deliberate close-up framings (a tighter flat illustration, not a lens
  change)
- top-down flat establishing illustrations

Avoid chaotic camera movement, and avoid claiming true 3D perspective shifts
that flat illustration can't actually deliver.

## Humor

Humor should come from relatable situations.

Prefer subtle visual reactions over exaggerated cartoon behavior.

## Human-Free Rule

No human actors by default.

Vehicles and environments should carry the story.
